# LESSONS: from source material to a traceable learning artifact

**Live project:** [LESSONS workspace](https://studio.jonathanrapisarda.dev/lessons)

## Problem

People often have useful notes spread across documents but lack a clear way to connect individual statements to the material they came from. Summaries can make interpretation look like established fact, making review harder.

## Workflow

1. Choose the built-in fictional example, paste authorized text, or supply a text or JSON source.
2. Synthesize a lesson from source-linked excerpts. The workspace presents evidence alongside interpretations and hypotheses.
3. Explore an optional question path and the knowledge graph to examine relationships among sources, statements and questions.
4. Export a readable lesson or a lesson with its graph. The public page also offers a workspace export for continuing work later.

The current page says selected source content stays in the browser tab rather than being uploaded to a shared Vault. Users remain responsible for choosing material they are authorized to process and protecting their exports.

## Fictional walkthrough

A team reviewing an imagined software handoff provides two invented notes: one says each release should name an owner, and another says the team should check the page before sharing a release. The workspace can connect those statements to their respective sources. A reader can then examine an interpretation about handoff clarity without treating it as an independently verified outcome.

This is an illustration of the workflow, not a client project or a measured result. The live workspace supplies its own fictional example.

## Evidence and limits

The public page describes a local, source-linked workflow with up to 80 adaptive questions. Its present synthesis is deterministic, and model-connected synthesis and hosted persistence are listed as pending. A source link helps a reader inspect a statement; it does not independently establish that the statement is true.
